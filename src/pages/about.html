Why these charts exist

I learned to crochet from free patterns, and I finished a lot of things that didn't fit. A hat that slid off. A blanket that ran out of yarn four rows from the end. A glasses case my mother couldn't actually get her glasses into.

None of those were skill problems. They were measurement problems — and nobody had written the measurements down in one place. So I started writing them down, and this site is where they live.

Rosie Payne

What this site is

A small set of crochet reference charts, kept deliberately plain: no pop-ups, no auto-playing video, no scrolling past nine paragraphs of preamble to reach the table. Every page is built to be read on a phone beside a project bag, or printed and kept in it.

There are five pages and there will not be many more. A reference you can hold in your head is more useful than an archive you have to search.

Where the numbers come from

Three sources, in this order:

  • Published industry standards for the things that are standardised — the yarn weight categories 0 to 7, their gauge ranges, and the hook size equivalences all follow the Craft Yarn Council's published tables.
  • Manufacturer ball bands and hook markings for what is actually sold, which is not always what the standards say. Where the two disagree, the disagreement is worth knowing about, so I note it rather than smoothing it over.
  • Swatching for everything else. The finished sizes, the ease allowances and the yardage figures come from making the things and measuring them.

Where a number is genuinely uncertain, the chart says so. The hook chart has empty cells in the UK column because two of the US sizes have no standard UK equivalent, and printing a plausible-looking number there would be more harmful than leaving the gap.

Which means these charts can still be wrong for you

Every figure here assumes an average that nobody actually is. Two crocheters working the same pattern, in the same yarn, with the same hook, will produce different fabric — tension is as personal as handwriting.

So treat these numbers as a starting point and your swatch as the authority. Where they disagree, your swatch is right. The calculators exist precisely so you can replace my averages with your own measurements.

Corrections

If a number here is wrong, it should be fixed rather than defended. Conversions get mis-transcribed, manufacturers change their ranges, and standards are revised.

Every page carries the date it was last checked, at the foot. If something does not match what you are holding in your hands, trust what you are holding.

About the books

These charts cover the measurements. The books cover what goes wrong in between — the dropped stitch six rows down, the foundation chain that fights you, the gauge that refuses to match no matter which hook you try.

They are where the longer tables these pages were drawn from are published in full, and buying one is what keeps this site free of advertising.
